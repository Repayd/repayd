Opening · about 25 seconds

Agents need room to act, but an open wallet is too much authority. REPAYD connects three things: an owner's spending policy, a record of how a decision was made, and a pool that can pay a covered loss. We do not claim perfect prevention. We demonstrate a boundary that works, a deliberately controlled failure of the watcher, and an actual testnet recovery payment.

Say explicitly: This is an ETHOnline prototype using mintable Demo USDC on Arc testnet. No customer traction, production insurance, audited security, or guaranteed recovery is being represented.

02 / 16

The problem · about 25 seconds

A payment agent reads things it did not author: websites, tools, invoices. The failure we care about is when that content is promoted into payment authority. Detection is important, but detecting something after settlement does not put the funds back. The product question is not just “can we block this?” It is “what can execute, what must wait, and what happens if a covered mistake still settles?”

Do not overclaim: The demo uses supplied instruction scenarios; it is not a live external attack. We are not claiming that prompt injection explains all agent losses, or assigning an unsupported frequency to it.

04 / 16

The mechanism · about 35 seconds

The owner installs the rules. The guard applies hard limits first, then classifies the remaining payment. Routine payments execute; unfamiliar recipients are held; violations revert. The watcher can sign a release or a freeze. A freeze already stops this payment—owner action is an override or cancellation, not a prerequisite for protection. A covered-loss verdict is a separate message that calls the pool in the same transaction.

If asked about timing: The demo policy has a 120-second review window, but a clean verdict need not wait the full two minutes. Silence is not automatic permission to pay. The owner can resolve a hold, and the lapse path can extend and then freeze it. The new demo deliberately broadcasts the 900-unit violation, producing a real reverted receipt and gas cost; do not repeat the old report's “no transaction exists” description.

05 / 16

Why the lookalike matters · about 35 seconds

The guard is not fooled by the lookalike. It compares full addresses and holds both unknown recipients. The difference happens in the watcher. The fresh case scores one point for being unknown, one for no history, and one for the simulated unusual hour: three, so freeze. For the lookalike we deliberately supply a thirty-day history and noon activity. Only the unknown-recipient point remains, so the watcher releases. That is the controlled false negative.

If asked why not deny all new recipients: For a fixed payroll list, exact allowlisting may be enough. Service-buying agents need a bounded path to legitimate new vendors. We do not pretend that this heuristic proves safety. The hold is on chain; the score and the facts used here are local, constructed inputs. No live reputation oracle or TEE is claimed.

06 / 16

Trust, precisely · about 35 seconds

We bind the rules, the recorded instruction, and the authority that signed the verdict. Those are useful guarantees, but they are not interchangeable with truth. EIP-712 authenticates a message and its domain. It does not independently verify a token loss or prove that captured instructions are complete. Today, the demo runner checks receipts and balances before its configured local watcher signs the claim.

If challenged on the “alibi”: An owner-signed record can support denial if the capture path is authenticated and the signing key is isolated. The SDK's caller supplies origin; a hash chain alone cannot prevent selective capture, a rebuilt history, or a compromised key. This demo has no TEE cosignature and no external commitment proving capture completeness. The current contract trusts the watcher-reported loss. Covered-claim validation and hold-release validation are separate paths; do not imply every hold policy field is independently re-evaluated on chain.

07 / 16

The two moments · about 40 seconds

First, four ordinary payrolls move 180, 310, 90 and 220—800 total. The fresh 150-unit payment is held and frozen; nothing leaves. A 900-unit proposal produces a genuine reverted transaction. Then the lookalike is also held. We deliberately make the watcher miss by supplying benign history. A signed clean release really sends 150 to that address. Only after checking the transfer and balance deltas do we submit a covered verdict that pays the owner 135.

The distinction that matters: This is not the stale historical sequence where a held proposal stood in for an actual loss. Release and claim are separate transactions. The 135 is paid by the pool, not clawed back from the recipient. Expected final snapshots are guard 3,050; owner 135; lookalike 150; fresh 0; junior 19,865; senior 25,000; daily spend 950 and count 5. Those are the programmed expectations here, not a claim that this deck has attached a completed run yet.

09 / 16

Economic discipline · about 40 seconds

The demo seeds 45,000 mintable tokens: 20,000 junior, 25,000 senior. Junior takes the 135-unit claim, leaving 19,865; senior stays at 25,000. The tariff is separate arithmetic: a 2,500-dollar cap times two percent per month is 50; the SDK factor brings that to 45; a first-claim factor of three produces 135 per month, with other factors neutral. The identical number 135 for payout and premium is a coincidence, not a linkage.

Defensible boundaries: Two percent is monthly, not annual: the neutral 50 monthly quote is 600 over twelve months, or 24% of the cap on a simple, non-compounded basis. This has not been calibrated to loss data. The engine computes the 10% deductible; the contract checks bounds and trusts the signed reported loss, rather than reconstructing that loss or recomputing the exact engine formula. A recordPremium transfer helper exists; automated recurring debits and premium distribution are not demonstrated. Forty-five thousand divided by a 2,500 cap is eighteen cap-sized claims before other flows—not a solvency model.

12 / 16

Differentiation · about 25 seconds

Wallet controls answer what may execute. Detection answers whether an action looks suspicious. Neither function alone answers how an eligible loss is settled. Our distinction is the connected workflow: execution controls, a recorded decision trail, and conditional capital settlement. We can use other detection and wallet infrastructure; this is not an argument that those layers are useless.

If asked about competitors or defensibility: We are not claiming “first,” “only,” or zero competitors. The defensibility we would have to earn is reliable capture and adjudication, useful integration at wallet creation, better measured loss experience, and disciplined capital management. The historical hackathon-corpus counts are not market evidence and are intentionally absent.

13 / 16

The next proof to earn · about 35 seconds

A working conditional payout is not a finished insurer. First, capture must be authenticated and keys isolated; an attestation needs a verifiable trust chain, not a successful HTTP response. Second, the claim must be bound to trustworthy settlement evidence and contract enforcement must match the intended payout rule. Third, rates and aggregate exposure need real loss data and correlated-loss stress. Finally, we need security and regulatory review, refreshed integrations, and authorized collection.

Be precise about gaps: The watcher currently reports the loss to the contract. The demo runner checks that loss, but those checks are not independent on-chain proof. A per-claim cap does not establish a policy-period aggregate limit or portfolio solvency. Reinsurance is a future hook, not live backing. Premium collection has a helper, not an end-to-end recurring authorization product. Do not equate this with deposit protection, a guarantee, or audited production readiness.

15 / 16

Q&A · control and trust

“Is this an if/else allowlist?” — 30-second answer: The guard deliberately uses a simple exact match; that is the correct primitive for fixed payroll recipients. A one-character lookalike is not allowlisted. The product work is the path for new payees, an inspectable decision, and conditional recovery if an authorized release is wrong. Known payees still face hard caps and other policy conditions. A recipient sub-cap can replace the global per-transfer cap: that is why Bob's 310 payment fits the demo's explicit 400 sub-cap despite the general 200 limit.

“What if the watcher lies or disappears?” — 30-second answer: Its current signature is trusted authority, not independent truth. It can make a bad release decision, which the demo intentionally shows. No verdict is not automatic permission to pay; pending holds have a lapse/freeze path, and frozen funds do not transfer without an authorized action. Production needs isolated signing, trustworthy data, and independently defensible claim evidence.

“Why can't the owner manufacture an external instruction?” — 30-second answer: The current local SDK alone cannot rule that out. It verifies a recorded owner signature and chain integrity, but the caller supplies origin and capture completeness is not independently proved. A fully authenticated capture boundary and isolated owner key are required to make attribution meaningful. We demonstrate a local owner-origin denial; we do not claim universal first-party-fraud prevention.

16 / 16

Q&A · claims, premiums and liquidity

“Is every bad payment insured?” — 30-second answer: No. The demonstrated engine path requires external provenance and a defined policy breach after a settled loss. Owner-origin decisions are denied in the local example. The contract then validates the authorized message, current covered-claim policy, claimant, cap and outcome consistency; it does not independently prove the loss or recompute the full engine decision. The runner supplies actual receipt and balance verification before signing.

“Who pays the premium, and is 2% annual?” — 30-second answer: The owner or platform would pay for coverage. The prototype rate is two percent of the cap per month before factors, not per year. At a 2,500 cap that is 50 monthly, then 45 with the SDK factor, then 135 after the illustrative first-claim factor. The quote is uncalibrated. recordPremium can collect an approved token transfer; we have not demonstrated end-to-end recurring authorization, automatic collection or continuous per-block debiting. API fees on Hedera are not that premium.

“What happens if the pool runs dry?” — 30-second answer: The current payout consumes junior then senior capital. If capital is insufficient, it reverts; the enclosing verdict transaction does not finalize a paid claim. No partial payout queue, bailout or deployed reinsurance should be inferred. Coverage is conditional, not a solvency guarantee. Before production we must manage aggregate exposure, withdrawals and correlated losses, calibrate rates, obtain real backing and complete legal review.
